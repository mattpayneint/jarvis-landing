How Fast Should Attorneys Respond to a New Client Inquiry?

A family calls, or gets handed your name, and what happens in the next day decides more than most firms realize. Here's what the research says about that window, and why I think the real fix starts earlier.

By Matthew Payne · Founder, Matthew Payne Consulting

The Short Answer

  • Most people won't wait long. A 2025 Scorpion survey found 72% of prospective legal clients move on if they don't hear back within 24 hours.
  • The shopping window is short to begin with. A 2023 Martindale-Avvo report found 55% of legal consumers decide within two weeks, and nearly 1 in 4 decide within three days. Half said they'll contact two to four attorneys before choosing one.
  • Reviews now carry real weight. The same Scorpion survey found nearly 60% of consumers say online reviews carry more weight than word of mouth, and over half won't consider a firm rated below four stars.
  • None of this is HNW-specific, so I wouldn't call it a finding about this practice area. The real fix is the actual cause: too many inquiries start that clock with someone who's never heard of you.

A family calls, or fills out a form, or gets handed your name by their CPA, and what happens in the next day decides more than most attorneys act like it does. Most firms treat response time as an administrative detail. The research says it's closer to the whole ballgame for a certain kind of inquiry.

Scorpion's 2025 consumer study and Martindale-Avvo's reports from 2022 and 2023 all studied general legal consumers, not estate planning or business law specifically, and none of it is limited to families with real net worth. I haven't found research that measures response time or shopping behavior for high-net-worth legal clients on their own, so I'd treat the figures as the general shape of the problem, and my read on what to do about it as judgment built on top of them.

The window is shorter than most firms act like it is

Scorpion's 2025 consumer research found that 72% of potential clients will move on if they don't hear back within 24 hours. Separately, a 2023 Martindale-Avvo report on legal consumer behavior found that 55% of people decide within two weeks of starting their search, and nearly 1 in 4 decide in three days or less. Half said they'll contact two to four attorneys before making a final decision, and responsiveness was among the three most important factors they named.

Reviews are starting to outweigh word of mouth

The same Scorpion research found that nearly 60% of consumers say online reviews now carry more weight than word of mouth, and that more than half won't consider a firm rated below four stars. A separate 2022 Martindale-Avvo study found that 34% of legal consumers start with a search engine, and that nearly 70% use online content, including profiles and reviews, to understand their issue before they ever pick up the phone. By the time someone calls, they've usually already looked you up.

Why I don't think the fix is a faster intake script

The obvious response to all of this is operational: answer faster, automate the first reply, hire someone to own intake. Those are reasonable moves, and I wouldn't talk anyone out of them. But they treat the symptom. A family running the two-to-four-firm clock found you cold, through a search or a directory, with no relationship to slow them down while they compare.

A family that already knows you, through a CPA who's been mentioning you for a year, or through a conversation you had with them directly before they needed anything, isn't running that clock the same way. They're not holding your name against three others from a search result. That 24 hour window matters most to an attorney a stranger just found, and barely at all to one a family already trusts.

Common Questions

Does a fast response guarantee a new client?

No. Scorpion's 2025 research measured how many people say they'll move on without a reply, not what actually wins the matter. A fast response removes one reason to lose someone you'd otherwise have kept. It doesn't replace fit or trust.

Is this data specific to estate planning or business law?

No. Both surveys covered general legal consumers, and I haven't found research that separates out high-net-worth families or business owners specifically. I'd treat the numbers as the general shape of the problem, not a finding about this practice area.

Should attorneys compete on online reviews?

I wouldn't build a strategy around it, but I also wouldn't ignore what the Scorpion data shows about how much weight reviews carry now. My own preference is still relationships built peer to peer, because that's what I've spent twenty years on and what I see working for my clients.

What should a firm do if it can't respond within 24 hours?

Fix what you can operationally, and also ask a different question: how many of these inquiries are arriving from people who've never heard of you before today. The fewer of those you have, the less that 24 hour window controls the outcome.
