How Do GPs Get Fewer, Better-Qualified LP Meetings?

A full calendar of first calls with LPs feels like progress. The data on how LPs actually find managers says a lot of those calls were never going anywhere.

By Matthew Payne · Founder, Matthew Payne Consulting

The Short Answer

  • LPs mostly aren't discovering managers through a cold list. iConnections' Global Allocator Report 2026 found nearly 80% of LPs say they find new managers through their own professional networks, and over half cite conferences and events as a primary channel.
  • Capital keeps concentrating at the top. Preqin found just over half of 2024's committed capital went to the 100 biggest funds, up from more than a third in 2021.
  • The raise itself is already harder. Bain's Global Private Equity Report 2026 found the number of funds closed fell for a fourth straight year, and called fundraising a grind for many GPs.
  • So I'd judge a week less by how many LP calls got booked and more by how many of them could actually go somewhere.

A full calendar of first calls with LPs feels like progress. For a lot of GPs I talk with, it's treated as the goal itself: book more intro meetings, keep the pipeline full. I think that instinct is aimed at the wrong number.

I'm not a placement agent, I'm not licensed, and I don't raise or place capital for anyone. What follows is my read of a few numbers I think every GP should sit with before the next raise, not a claim about any particular LP or fund.

LPs say they find managers through relationships, not inboxes

iConnections' Global Allocator Report 2026 reports that nearly 80% of LPs say they discover new managers through their own professional networks, and over half cite conferences and industry events as a primary sourcing channel. The report describes databases and cold contact as a much smaller share. iConnections sells conferences and introductions to allocators and managers, so it has a stake in that answer, and the page doesn't give a sample size or say whether respondents could pick more than one source. Even with that caveat, it lines up with what I've watched happen on the ground. The GPs who get real conversations are usually already a step inside the network before the ask is made.

Capital is concentrating while fewer funds get raised

Preqin's research found that in 2024, just over half of all committed capital went to the 100 biggest funds, up from more than a third in 2021. Bain's Global Private Equity Report 2026 found total private capital raised was roughly flat at about $1.3 trillion, buyout fundraising fell 16%, and the number of funds closed fell for a fourth straight year, in almost every category. Bain's own language calls fundraising a grind for many general partners.

Put those two together and the math on a long list of generic LP meetings gets worse. More capital is landing with fewer, larger funds, and more managers are competing for what's left over. A GP spending the week on calls with LPs who were never going to write a check is burning scarce calendar time on a shrinking pool.

Why I'd screen the meeting before it's booked, not during it

The usual fix is to meet more people, on the logic that volume covers for a low hit rate. I'd go the other way. If LPs themselves say they trust their own networks over a cold list, the real job is to already be inside more of the right networks before the ask happens, not to widen the list.

That means knowing, before a call lands on the calendar, whether the fund sits within that investor's mandate, whether they're actually deploying new capital right now, and whether the person you'd be meeting can move anything forward. That kind of read on a fund comes from a relationship that already exists before the raise does, not from a database. Building those relationships ahead of time is the part I do with GPs.

Common Questions

Does fewer meetings mean a slower raise?

Not in my experience. A GP's calendar is the scarce resource in a raise, and a meeting with someone outside your mandate or not currently deploying doesn't move anything forward, no matter how pleasant the conversation is.

Is contacting LPs cold a waste of time?

I haven't seen data that says contacting LPs cold doesn't work, and I wouldn't claim that. iConnections' report describes it as a smaller share of how LPs say they find managers, not a failing channel. What I'd change is where you spend the most scarce resource, which is your own calendar.

How do you know if an LP is actually deploying right now?

You usually can't know that from a cold list. It's the kind of question that comes up naturally inside a relationship that exists before the raise does, which is why I build those relationships ahead of time rather than during the pitch.

Is this data specific to venture capital, or does it cover other fund types?

The sources here cover private capital broadly, including private equity, buyout and private credit, not venture or syndications specifically. I haven't found data that separates out emerging managers or deal-by-deal sponsors, so I'd treat the pattern as directional.
